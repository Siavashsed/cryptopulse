## QUORA
**Answer this question:** How do funding rates work in crypto perpetual futures and why do they matter for traders?

Funding rates are the mechanism that keeps perpetual futures prices tethered to spot prices. Here's how they actually work:

Perpetual futures contracts have no expiry date, unlike standard futures. This creates a problem: without an expiration forcing convergence, the perpetual price can drift significantly from the spot price. Exchanges solve this with periodic funding payments between long and short holders.

When there's excessive bullish bias (too many longs), the funding rate becomes positive and longs pay shorts. When sentiment flips bearish, the rate goes negative and shorts pay longs. This creates an economic incentive to rebalance positions.

Why it matters: Most retail traders ignore this entirely. On May 19, 2021, Bitcoin perp funding rates hit negative 0.75% per 8-hour interval—roughly 820% annualized on a long position. If you were holding longs that day, you were hemorrhaging capital from funding alone, independent of the price collapse from $43k to $30k.

For algorithmic traders and systematic strategies, funding rates are a separate profit/loss stream you must price in. They can make or break your edge depending on positioning and market regime.
