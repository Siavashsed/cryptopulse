## LINKEDIN
**Hook:** A $1 billion USDT mint usually tells you about last month, not next week.

Most crypto traders treat treasury mint alerts as a buy signal. The logic sounds clean: new stablecoins mean new dry powder, and dry powder eventually buys something.

In practice, mints and total supply lag. Many mints fill demand that was already arranged off exchange, and some sit as treasury inventory before they move at all. The signal arrives after the decision was made.

The slice that does lead is narrower: net stablecoin deposits onto spot exchanges, measured over days rather than hours. That is capital sitting where it can actually be spent. Hourly data is mostly wallets being shuffled around.

I learned this with a 3x long on BTC perps, placed ten minutes after a mint alert in March 2024. It was an expensive lesson with a simple point: measure where the money goes, not where it gets printed.

## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Do stablecoin inflows predict Bitcoin price movements?

Partly. Only one slice of stablecoin flow has real leading value, though, and it is not the one most people watch.

There are three common measures:

1. Total stablecoin supply. It trends with the market, but it moves slowly and mostly confirms what already happened. Supply rising over a quarter tells you liquidity grew. It does not tell you what happens next week.

2. Treasury mints. The '1,000,000,000 USDT minted' alerts feel like a signal. But a mint often covers demand that institutional or OTC clients arranged beforehand, and some minted supply sits in the issuer's treasury as inventory before it goes anywhere. The alert is the receipt, not the order.

3. Net stablecoin deposits onto spot exchanges. This is the useful one. Stablecoins that arrive on a spot venue are capital positioned to buy. In my experience, net inflows that stay high for several days have come before spot buying pressure far more reliably than mints or supply.

Two practical details matter. The first is the time window. Hourly netflows are dominated by noise like internal wallet rotation and market maker rebalancing, so use multi-day windows. The second is the venue mix. Deposits to derivatives venues behave differently from spot deposits because they often serve as margin, not purchasing power.

I learned the mint lesson the expensive way. In March 2024 I went long BTC perps at 3x within ten minutes of a $1 billion USDT mint alert, treating it as a buy signal. It was not one.
