## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Which is better for crypto trading: a grid bot or a trend-following bot?

Neither is better in the abstract. They're opposite bets on the same market, and the right one depends on the current regime.

A grid bot places buy and sell orders at fixed intervals inside a price range. It profits from oscillation: every small swing up and down gets harvested. In a sideways market it can feel like a steady paycheck. The weakness is a breakout. If price leaves your range to the upside, the bot has sold everything early and sits idle. If it drops below, you're left holding a full bag of the asset at a loss.

A trend-following bot does the opposite. It waits for direction, enters after a move is established, and rides it with a trailing exit. In choppy markets it takes lots of small losses from whipsaws. Its profits come in bursts when a real trend shows up, and those few large wins have to cover all the small losses.

I learned this firsthand. In March 2024 I ran a Binance grid bot on ETH/USDT with $5,000 and 40 grids between $3,200 and $3,900. It earned around $11 a day for two weeks. Then ETH ran through the top of the range and the bot just stopped earning while the market kept moving without it.

My practical advice:

1. Identify the regime first. Is price ranging or trending? Look at volatility and how often price is revisiting the same levels.
2. Pick the bot built to win in that regime.
3. Define in advance the condition that tells you to turn it off.
4. Size positions so a regime change doesn't hurt you badly.
