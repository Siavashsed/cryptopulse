## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Why do liquidation cascades happen so often in crypto markets?

They happen often because the structure of leveraged crypto trading almost guarantees them. It isn't mainly about panic or manipulation.

The key point is that every leveraged position has its exit price fixed before the trade opens. Your entry price and leverage decide your liquidation level. That forced seller never gets to choose a price. When the market touches the level, the exchange closes the position at market, whatever liquidity is there.

The forced sale is what starts the chain. A large market sell pushes price down, and that brings price into the next group of liquidation levels. Those positions are closed at market too, which pushes price further, and the process feeds itself until it runs out of fuel or hits a deep enough wall of bids.

Three things make crypto especially prone to this:

1. Leverage clusters. Many traders open similar leverage (5x, 10x, 20x) off the same recent highs, lows or round numbers. So liquidation levels stack up in tight bands instead of spreading out.

2. Liquidity is uneven. Order books thin out overnight and on weekends, so each forced fill moves price further.

3. Leverage is easy to get. High leverage on perpetual futures is one click away, and open interest can build quickly.

I learned this firsthand on May 19, 2021, holding a 5x ETH long during that crash. Since then I've treated leverage data as a map of where the market is fragile. The signs I watch are open interest rising faster than spot volume, funding staying positive for a long time, and dense liquidation levels close to the current price. They won't tell you timing, but they show you where a cascade would get its fuel. I wrote a longer breakdown of the mechanics here if you want more detail: https://siavashsed.github.io/cryptopulse/liquidation-cascades-leverage-predictable-price-moves/

The practical takeaway is to size so that your liquidation level sits well outside the obvious clusters, or skip high leverage altogether.
