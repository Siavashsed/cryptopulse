## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Why does algorithmic trading outperform emotional trading in volatile crypto markets?

Short answer: a script doesn't have a stomach.

I spent years as a quant trader, and the most expensive lesson I ever learned was on March 12, 2020. Bitcoin dropped from roughly $7,900 to under $5,000 in a single session. At 3:40 a.m., tired and shaking, I market-sold a $60,000 position by hand. Meanwhile a grid bot on a separate account kept buying every level on the way down and was positioned for the rebound. Same market, same day, very different outcomes.

Here is why that gap shows up so reliably:

1. Fear peaks when prices are worst. Human traders tend to sell into forced-liquidation cascades, which is precisely when prices are most dislocated. A bot executes the rules it was given, regardless of how ugly the chart looks.

2. Fatigue and timing. Crypto trades 24/7. Humans sleep, get distracted and make poor decisions at 3 a.m. Algorithms don't degrade over a long session.

3. No revenge trading. After a loss, people often size up to win it back. A properly built system follows its position limits every single time.

4. Speed and consistency. Orders are placed in milliseconds, and the same logic is applied to the thousandth trade as to the first.

It's not all upside, though. Bots are only as good as their design. A grid strategy can accumulate heavy exposure in a persistent downtrend, and bad parameters or missing risk caps simply automate mistakes. Backtest honestly, include fees and slippage, size positions conservatively, and set a maximum drawdown that shuts the system off.

The practical takeaway is to make your decisions when you're calm, encode them as rules, and let the machine carry them out when you're not.
